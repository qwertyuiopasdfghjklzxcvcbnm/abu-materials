| S08 | 697–850 | c194–242 | What traders talk about. (1) Support/resistance in many forms: moving averages (average price as resistance in a strong selloff, support in a strong rally) and channels (a bear channel after a bull breakout is probably a bull flag). (2) Prior highs/lows flipping between resistance and support. (3) Trend strength: higher highs/lows vs lower highs/lows. Example: bear trend, strong bull breakout, higher low, major trend reversal into a bull trend. |
| S09 | 857–950 | c243–265 | Two forces that control markets: context and momentum. Context: a line across the tops acts as resistance even when briefly exceeded. Sell pullbacks near the trend line with a stop below the prior bar. Prior lows act as support; buy reversals with a stop above the prior bar's high (green boxes mark the entries). Value-investor logic: buy cheap, sell expensive. |
| S10 | 950–1166 | c266–331 | Momentum = trend strength. Trade in its direction: you can sell the close of a strong bear bar or buy the close of a strong bull bar. Buy the breakout or the pullback; traders sell all the way down. Loss of momentum (small bear bars, tails, bull reversal bars) moves a strong bear trend toward a weak bear trend, then a range (double bottom), then maybe a bull trend. Reasonable reversal: double bottom / lower-low MTR after a strong break above the bear channel plus a test of the low. Early strength: 4 consecutive big bull bars = buying pressure. Selling pressure: lower highs and lower lows bar after bar. Probability and trade management matter as much as risk/reward. |
| S11 | 1168–1217 | c332–346 | Candlestick patterns are unnecessary. Many institutions trade line charts. On a line chart with higher highs and lows you buy, and no candle pattern would change that. |
| S12 | 1218–1444 | c347–413 | Indicators. Old magazine charts were stuffed with indicators. The same chart with indicators removed shows a series of price-action reads (failed range breakout and big reversal bar: sell; three pushes down and a range breakout below: buy; small triangle inside a bigger triangle: sell; failed bull reversal in a strong bear trend: sell; minor reversal, possible final flag, decent buy reversal bar: buy; 50% pullback of a selloff, second entry, small double top: sell; small triangle with failed bull breakout: sell; higher-low MTR with two legs down: buy). Indicators show none of this. Indicators make him hunt reversals and breakouts, the opposite of what he should do. Bullish double divergence inside a tight bear channel / small-pullback bear trend: only selling makes money there. Indicators are an excuse not to see the obvious. |
| S13 | 1444–1566 | c413–454 | Risk/reward vs probability. Buying in a strong bear trend has tiny risk and huge reward but very low probability. Great risk/reward implies the institution on the other side has high probability. Many small losses add up. Be patient; there are no shortcuts; competitors are smart and want every penny. |
| S14 | 1567–1638 | c455–477 | Chart type and timeframe. A EUR/JPY monthly chart looks like any intraday chart. The process is the same on every timeframe because markets are fractal. Monthly bars are big, so stops are far and size must be small to keep per-trade risk equal. |
